Discovery & Homepage Rethink

Why this is cluster 07. Tony's strategy memo (Apr 30) was clear: market fit isn't there. The bottleneck is that after a user exhausts the top 60 lists, discovery falls off a cliff. Sophia and Sydny independently said the hierarchy should flip — find people first, then their lists. The bar for "wow" on a personalization-first product (camera roll, location, social graph) has risen dramatically.

This is the cluster where I'd be most careful not to sprint into a build. The shape of the answer matters more than the speed. Recommendation: pick 2–3 directions to prototype side-by-side over 3 weeks. Then commit.

Strategy doc · 3-week prototype sprint

Don't pick the answer yet. Build three competing first-session experiences in lo-fi, test with cohorts, commit to one in week 4.

The thesis

Three real candidate hypotheses are visible in the backlog. Each implies a different product. Each is defensible. We don't have data to pick yet — and tactical homepage redesigns won't surface that data because the question is structural, not visual.

The three candidates

  • H1: People-first. The home is a feed of people. You follow people, you trust their lists. Lists are subordinate. (Sophia + Sydny + Tony's "human connection is the differentiator.")
  • H2: Interest-first / Pinterest-style. Onboarding asks for interests; home is For-You'd content tied to declared + implied interests. (Tony Apr 30 + ID 102 + ID 43.)
  • H3: Vertical-first. The site/app is multiple verticals (beauty, food, travel, parenting). Each has its own home, its own creators, its own SEO surface. (ID 47 + Vertical landing prototype with 21 thread replies.)

Sprint structure (3 weeks)

  1. Week 1: Lo-fi prototypes. Three clickable Figma/HTML prototypes, one per hypothesis, sharing the same content seed. Skip features; focus on home + onboarding + 1 deep flow.
  2. Week 2: Cohort testing. 8–12 users per cohort, ideally split between active and lapsed. 30-minute sessions. Measure: 3-day return intent, "what would bring you back tomorrow," and the "wow" question.
  3. Week 3: Convergence + write up. Pick one (or hybrid). Write the Discovery v2 PRD, including the parts of the other two we keep.

What to ship in parallel (no-regret moves)

  • ID 44 · Search quality overhaul. 1-2 days. Should already be in flight (Logan).
  • ID 50 · AEO structure for rec pages. Independent of the discovery question. Free SEO upside.
  • ID 110 · Stories removal toggle. Lightweight, lets us cut clip-disabled users out of clip noise without the larger removal decision.

Open product decisions

D-14 · Stories — keep, hide, or remove?
Option A · User-level toggle, default off for new users
Lets engaged story-fans keep them; reduces noise for everyone else. Cleanest path to focusing the platform without burning the feature outright. Recommended.
Option B · Remove entirely
Strongest focus signal. Loses any story content as a discovery surface. Unrecoverable.
Option C · Keep as-is
Status quo. Stories add visual noise but cost little to keep. Default if no clear signal otherwise.
D-15 · Vertical strategy — separate product or homepage variants?
Option A · Separate vertical sites for SEO + acquisition only
Lightest. Marketing surface, not a real product fork. Good for AEO + ads. Doesn't change the core experience.
Option B · One product, vertical-aware home (per signed-in user)
Same app, but the home reflects declared verticals. Aligns with H2 above. Right shape for personalization. Recommended.
Option C · True vertical product split (separate apps)
Most ambitious; potentially fragments creators. Probably wrong unless one vertical genuinely doesn't fit the rest.
D-16 · Should we delay this to Q4 in favor of UGC Studio + Brand Portal?
Option A · Yes — discovery is the consumer side; revenue is brand-side
Aligns capacity with the fastest revenue. Risks consumer side stagnating; may damage long-term brand-side appeal because discovery is what brings creators.
Option B · Run the 3-week sprint now in parallel; commit Q4
Cheap parallel research now, full commitment when capacity opens. Recommended.
Option C · Treat discovery as the #1 priority; defer UGC Studio
Bets on consumer-side rebuild. Probably wrong given UGC Studio's stronger validation.
