Creator Onboarding & Profile

Why this is cluster 04. Two problems share a screen: (1) ~50% of creators are getting rejected because the brand portal asks for things onboarding didn't capture, and (2) creators with strong fit are filtered out because their profile doesn't show their range — engagement, content, brand-collab history. The fix is a single, slightly-richer onboarding plus a profile/portfolio surface that brands actually use to decide.

This is the highest-leverage matching quality work in the backlog. Not a moonshot, but quietly raises every campaign-side metric for free.

Product doc · Onboarding v2 + Profile v1

Two surfaces, one motion. Capture the data we need at signup; show that data back as a creator profile brands trust.

The opportunity

Today, ~50% of brand-portal invites are rejected during a second email round because we asked the question too late. Meanwhile, brands looking at creator pools see a follower count, an avatar, and not much else — they can't tell a 3K-follower nano with strong engagement apart from a bot. Both halves of that gap are the same fix: capture more in onboarding, show more on the profile.

Target users

  • Creator (signing up). Wants to get into campaigns fast. Currently bounces on long onboarding; currently gets disqualified later.
  • Brand (browsing pool). Wants signal beyond follower count. Today gives up on small creators because they can't tell who's serious.

Onboarding v2 scope

  • Source-aware first screens (ID 34). Detect entry point (TTC, organic, event, brand invite) and pick the first 2–3 questions accordingly.
  • Add the rejection-causing questions early (ID 61). Gifted openness · paid willingness · brand-category blocks · response time SLA. These are exactly the questions Sienna emails about today.
  • Conditional reveals (already in v1). Keep.
  • Editable post-submission (ID 78). Dot-dot-dot → re-enter the flow with answers prefilled.
  • "Why brands should pick you" prompt (ID 48). Optional. See decision below.

Profile v1 scope (what a brand sees)

  • Header: avatar, name, location, follower count, intro video (ID 33).
  • "What I'm into" — categories, brand affinities, tone of voice signals.
  • Brand work — past collaborations with thumbnails (uses Cluster 06 link library).
  • Engagement signals — engagement rate, posting cadence, audience location share.
  • Brand ratings — once we have a few campaigns under each creator (ID 141, gated to 3+ completed campaigns).
  • "Open to" badges — gifted / paid / hybrid / UGC-only.

Profile is the source of truth that surfaces in: brand-side creator pool, public-facing share link (ID 31), and AI-generated creator cards (ID 132).

Open product decisions

D-09 · "Why brands should pick you" — friction or filter?
Option A · Optional, but unlocks a "verified" badge after review
Adds quality signal without rejecting creators who skip. Verified badge becomes a useful filter for brands. Recommended.
Option B · Required for all new creators
Filters strongly for serious creators. Risks losing nanos who'd be high-fit but don't articulate well. Real cost: bounce rate.
Option C · Cut entirely
Lowest friction. Loses a useful trust signal at exactly the moment we need it.
D-10 · Brand ratings on profiles (ID 141) — show to other brands?
Option A · Aggregate score visible to brands; individual ratings hidden
"4.8/5 across 12 campaigns" is a useful signal without weaponizing single bad ratings. Most platforms (Airbnb, Upwork) work this way. Recommended.
Option B · Individual ratings visible (with brand name)
Maximally transparent. Risk of one petty rating tanking a creator. Ratings game theory gets bad fast.
Option C · Ratings hidden, used internally for matching only
Safest for creators. Brands don't see what they want to see. Defeats the validation purpose.

UI exploration · onboarding shape

Two ways to handle the source-channel branching (ID 34). The whole point: someone arriving from a TTC video shouldn't see the same first screen as a brand-invited creator.

Option A · Implicit branching, single visible flow Recommended
Welcome

Came from Tony's TikTok? Let's keep it short.

3 quick questions, then you're in. We'll ask the rest later.

What kind of brands do you want?
Beauty Fashion Wellness Food Travel
Open to gifted only?
Yes, gifted is fine Paid only Both
Anything you won't post about?
Why this: Branching happens behind the scenes — title and selected questions reflect the entry point. Creator never sees a "branch picker." Reads like Benable cares.
Option B · Explicit "tell us why you're here"
Welcome to Benable

What brings you here?

A brand invited me
Skip the basics, jump to the campaign.
I make content already
Set up your profile + collab prefs.
I want to share what I love
Just lists, no campaigns yet.
A friend invited me
See their lists first.
Why this: User declares intent explicitly. Best when source attribution is unreliable. Costs: an extra screen, and people often pick the "wrong" answer because the labels don't quite match their head.
