Brand Campaign Configuration

Why this is cluster 03. The bones of campaign creation — what brands can configure, what creators can pick from, how content/commission/fulfillment behave. Most of these are table-stakes: brands ask, "Why can't I X?" and we say, "Oh, that's hardcoded." Each individual fix is small; together they make the platform feel grown-up. Several already have backend deployed — UI is the gap.

Most ideas here are Build with clear scopes. The shape of campaign config is the open question — see decision D-07.

Product doc · Campaign Config v2

Bundle 8 small changes (IDs 13, 16, 17, 24, 56, 64, 67, 87, 91, 147, 148) into one well-shaped milestone instead of 8 individual tickets.

Why bundle

Each of these alone is a 3-line ticket. Together, they're "Campaign config v2" — a coherent story for the team and a noticeable feel-better moment for brands. Bundling forces a single design pass on the campaign-builder UI rather than 8 tactical patches that fight each other.

Scope

The campaign-builder gains five new configurable axes:

  • Content types per campaign: brand picks any combination of Story / Post / Reel / UGC. Benable assigns formats to creators or lets creators pick from the allowed set.
  • Content timelines per deliverable type: default per format, brand-overridable.
  • Product selection cap: default 2; brand can set 1–N.
  • Per-creator commission: default brand-wide rate; per-row override on the creator pool.
  • Fulfillment mode: shipped product / gift card / no fulfillment (UGC-only).

All of those need a usage-rights clause (ID 67) and a one-revision-round clause (ID 91) shown to creators on accept.

Out of scope (explicitly)

  • Creator scoring (ID 57) — separate spec, depends on creator pipeline (Cluster 02).
  • Affiliate network integrations (ID 19) — heavier, separate dependency.
  • Shopify Buy integration (ID 18) — separate eng track, already in flight.

Open product decisions

D-07 · Should the campaign builder become a wizard or stay form-based?
Option A · Form with progressive disclosure
One scrollable page. Defaults handle 80% of cases. Power users can expand. Inline editing (ID 60) becomes a natural fit. Recommended.
Option B · 4–5 step wizard
More guidance for new brands. Higher friction for experienced ones. More design work for back-navigation, save-and-resume.
Option C · "Templates first, customize after"
Pulls campaign templates (ID 14) front-and-center. Best at reducing setup time for repeat campaigns. Requires templates to ship first.
D-08 · Selection-cap warning UX (ID 147)
Option A · Soft "you can choose 2 of these" with disabled state on cards beyond cap
Doesn't feel like an error; sets expectation up front. Tap blocked card → tooltip "swap one of your picks first." Recommended.
Option B · Allow tapping a 3rd, prompt "swap or cancel"
Action-first; surfaces intent. More forgiving. Slightly more complex state.
Option C · Keep current toast warning
Status quo. Felt error-y in prototype review.

UI exploration · selection cap UX (ID 147 / 148)

✓

Why this: Honors the user's tap. Surfaces intent at the moment they want a third item. Adds a sheet to dismiss; more state but more forgiving.
Option C · Toast (current)

Eye Cream

Why this: Status quo. Reads like an error. Toast is gone in 3 seconds; user may not understand why their tap didn't take.
