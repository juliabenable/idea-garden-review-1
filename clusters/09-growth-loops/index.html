Product doc · Reactivation Autopilot

The single highest-leverage growth bet. We already know the variance — the math shows 2.86% is achievable. Closing the gap from 0.3% mass pushes to 2%+ targeted is millions of touchpoints worth converting.

The thesis

Reactivation outcomes are dominated by two variables: who you target and what you say. We currently send the same blast to everyone. Best-performing campaigns ("0 bonus today only") show the upside is real. We don't need new growth channels — we need the existing channel to stop being mass.

v1 scope

  • Segment by behavior: last visit, signup source, lists created, links clicked, brand affinities.
  • Copy library: 12–20 templates with variables. Tagged by segment fit, urgency level, brand-affinity tone.
  • Send selection: per-user, picks segment-fit highest-likelihood-template.
  • Always-on A/B: 5% of each segment gets random template; learnings feed back.
  • Frequency cap: per-user, never more than 1 reactivation/week. Hard limit.

Out of scope

  • True ML-trained model. Start with rule-based + bandit; ML in v2.
  • SMS / push reactivation. Email first; expand once email pattern works.

Success metrics

  • Average reactivation rate ≥ 2.0% across all segments (vs ~0.7% today).
  • ≥ 3 segment-template combos that consistently beat 3% — rotate as creative dulls.
  • Unsubscribe rate doesn't materially worsen.

Open product decisions

D-19 · Use existing email tool (Customer.io / Iterable / etc.) or build internal?
Option A · Existing tool with our segmentation feeding in
Tool handles deliverability, unsubscribes, A/B. We supply segments + templates. Recommended.
Option B · Internal build
Full control over targeting logic. Heavy engineering for table-stakes (deliverability, unsub). Probably wrong unless an existing tool can't represent the segments we need.

Product doc · Referral Momentum Multiplier

40% of signups already come from referrals — without any deliberate momentum design. Closing that loop is the next 40%.

Why it matters

Two facts: K-factor is 0.1 (not viral), but 40% of signups are referral-driven. That means we're getting referral lift entirely from the depth of the existing base, not from any per-user multiplier. Adding even small momentum mechanics would compound.

v1 scope

  • Streak rewards: 2nd successful referral within 14 days = double credit. 3rd within 14 days = visible badge + bonus.
  • Double-credit events: 48-hour windows tied to events / launches.
  • Personal share-art: auto-generated sharable cards with the referrer's lists, formatted for IG Stories.
  • Visible momentum: "You've earned X this month" + "Maya is 1 referral away from a streak — give her a nudge?"

Open product decisions

D-20 · Should referral rewards be financial credit or social/status?
Option A · Financial only (current)
Direct, measurable. Cost scales with success. Less differentiated.
Option B · Hybrid — small financial + visible status (badges, streak displays)
Best of both. Status is free to give; small dollar amount is real motivation. Recommended.
Option C · Status only — replace cash with badges/perks
Lower direct cost. Risky: cash referral incentives are proven; status alone is weaker for low-engagement users.
